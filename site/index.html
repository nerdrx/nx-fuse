01 / The approach

Trust is earned.
Joint by joint.

A camera losing one ankle should not take down your entire skeleton. The design keeps assistance selective and the original Pico estimate available.

01 — OBSERVE

More than one viewpoint.

Start with one camera. Expand to room and bed views after calibration. Each observation needs a timestamp, a common coordinate space, and a confidence estimate.

02 — CORRECT

Help where it makes sense.

Evaluate joints individually. Reject stale or implausible observations and blend eligible corrections. Headset and controller tracking remain authoritative.

03 — FALL BACK

Keep Pico in the loop.

When visibility drops, assistance fades back toward Pico. Camera processing belongs outside the VR tracking loop so a stalled camera cannot stall the headset.

02 / Real spaces

Your room isn't a studio.

Different views solve different visibility problems. Overlapping cameras can add depth; separate views can extend coverage. Both need calibration.

Space to move.

Begin with visible hips and feet in a room-facing view. Test drift correction, movement lag, and transitions before adding more complexity.

Space to unwind.

Overhead views and lying poses are research targets. Blankets, crossed legs, and hidden joints remain hard cases; unseen limbs need fallback.

03 / Build in the open

Proof before promises.

Small milestones, explicit limits, and repeatable comparisons. The destination is live assistance; this release builds the foundation.

A few honest answers

Before you plug anything in.

Does this improve my tracking today?

Not yet. The current framework lets you investigate fusion using simulated data. Real camera observations and a working WiVRn NX adapter must be implemented and validated before it can correct tracking in a game.

Can I use phone cameras or any number of webcams?

Those are possible future inputs, not confirmed supported devices. Practical camera count depends on capture support, USB or network bandwidth, synchronization, inference cost, and calibration quality. The first hardware milestone deliberately uses one camera.

What happens under a blanket?

A camera cannot reliably recover invisible limbs from an image alone. The intended behavior is to reduce camera influence and retain the Pico estimate, rather than confidently invent a pose.

Where does camera footage go?

The intended architecture processes camera input locally. This release has no camera capture or cloud video processing. Any future recording feature should be explicit and off by default.
